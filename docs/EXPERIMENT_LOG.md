## E4 Primary ViT-B/16 — MEASURED
Test spoilage macro-F1 is **0.9536** (accuracy 0.9608, ROC-AUC 0.9893), and produce macro-F1 is 1.0000. Details are in `docs/MODEL_CARD.md` and Tables T2–T3.

## Ablation (Table T4, Figure 09) — MEASURED, single seed
| ID | Model | Produce mF1 | Spoilage mF1 | Spoilage AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E0a | HSV colour histogram + logistic regression | 0.866 | 0.828 | 0.885 |
| E0b | Frozen ImageNet ViT-B/16 + logistic regression (linear probe) | 1.000 | 0.916 | 0.979 |
| E1 | ViT-S/16 fine-tuned, no augmentation | 1.000 | 0.926 | 0.985 |
| E2 | ViT-S/16 fine-tuned, conventional augmentation | 1.000 | 0.879 | 0.936 |
| E3 | ViT-S/16, conventional augmentation + 480 GAN images | 1.000 | 0.937 | 0.984 |
| E4 | ViT-B/16 fine-tuned, conventional augmentation (**deployed**) | 1.000 | **0.954** | **0.989** |

All ViT-S runs used lr 5e-5 (heads 5e-4), batch 32 and up to 10 epochs with patience 4.

Interpretation, limited to what the data supports:
- **Fine-tuning versus a frozen backbone:** E4 is about 4 points of spoilage macro-F1 above E0b, and E0b is about 9 points above colour histograms.
- **Augmentation, E1 versus E2: inconclusive.**
  - E2 reached its best validation score at **epoch 1** and early-stopped at epoch 5, with validation spoilage F1 swinging between 0.84 and 0.93.
  - The ViT-S learning rate was probably too high for that run. A single unstable seed cannot show that augmentation hurts.
- **GAN, E3 versus E2:** E3 scored higher (0.937 vs 0.879), and E3 trained stably to epoch 9.
  Against the stable no-augmentation run (E1, 0.926) the difference is **+0.011, which is within single-seed noise**. **No GAN benefit is claimed.**
- **Next step:** repeat E1–E3 with 3 seeds and a lower ViT-S learning rate (2e-5).

## Split sensitivity (Table T8) — MEASURED
The same ViT-S + augmentation configuration was trained on a **day-grouped** split, where no produce or capture day is shared across partitions apart from Carrot/Not_spoiled (only 2 dates).
- Test spoilage macro-F1 is **0.812**, compared with 0.879 on the session-grouped split (AUC 0.926 vs 0.936). On the day-grouped split, validation was 0.978 but test was 0.812.
- **Reading:** generalising to *unseen days* is noticeably harder, and the session-split figures (including the 0.954 headline) are optimistic about temporal generalisation. A day-grouped ViT-B run was not done.

## Noise robustness (Table T5, Figure 07) — MEASURED
The corruptions are generated deterministically in memory on the clean test images at 224², with a SHA-256 seed per image and level. No corrupted copy was ever used for training.

| Corruption | Level | No denoiser | Median 3×3 | DnCNN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian σ | 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.20 | 0.928 / 0.891 / 0.805 | 0.910 / 0.886 / 0.813 | 0.920 / 0.896 / **0.837** |
| Salt & pepper p | 0.02 / 0.05 / 0.10 | 0.919 / 0.859 / 0.776 | 0.936 / 0.936 / 0.934 | **0.943 / 0.939 / 0.935** |
| Speckle σ | 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5 | 0.917 / 0.844 / 0.800 | 0.901 / 0.836 / 0.788 | **0.921 / 0.858** / 0.799 |

Values are spoilage macro-F1; clean is 0.954. Produce type stays ≥0.96 macro-F1 in every condition.
- Spoilage is far more noise-sensitive than produce type.
- Denoising roughly fixes impulse noise. It slightly *hurts* at light Gaussian noise (0.920 vs 0.928), which is consistent with the concern that denoising removes fine cues.

## DnCNN (Table T7, Figure 08) — MEASURED, under-trained
- **Configuration:** depth 17, width 64, residual form `x̂ = y − R(y)`, MSE loss.
- **Training data:** 64² patches from **train** images only, with Gaussian σ~U(0.02, 0.2) or salt-and-pepper p~U(0.01, 0.1).
- **Budget:** 10 minutes, which gave **only 1,065 steps**. That is far fewer than a converged DnCNN needs.
- **Paired quality on 60 validation images**, where the clean reference is known so PSNR and SSIM are valid:
  - DnCNN beats the noisy input everywhere (for example Gaussian σ = 0.1 goes from 20.3 to 23.0 dB).
  - DnCNN is **worse than a 3×3 median filter** on PSNR and SSIM in every condition (for example 27.7 dB for the median filter). The median filter is unusually strong here because TR-6 images are mostly smooth plain background.
- **Conclusion:** the DnCNN is under-trained. Its downstream gains in T5 are modest, and it stays out of the inference path.

## GAN — MEASURED (training-only research experiment)
- **Model:** class-conditional DCGAN at 64×64 with 12 conditions (produce × spoilage), trained on the **train split only** for 41 epochs in 12 minutes (`artifacts/experiments/gan/`).
- **Visual review** (`samples_grid_rows_by_condition.png`):
  - Each condition is recognisable as its produce type, and Spoiled conditions are darker or browner for banana, carrot and guava.
  - The images are low resolution and blurry, and banana rows show low diversity (partial mode collapse).
- **Memorisation check:**
  - The median L2 distance from a generated image to its nearest training image of the same condition is 22.5.
  - The median distance between real training images is 12.8, because sessions contain near-identical views.
  - So the generated images are not near-copies of training images.
- 480 images were generated (40 per condition) and recorded in `generated_manifest.csv` as `synthetic_cdcgan_train_only`. They were used only in E3's training set.
- **FID:** not computed. With 480 images at 64² it would not be meaningful.

## K-means (Table T6, Figure 10) — MEASURED
- **Method:**
  - Features are the frozen ImageNet ViT-B/16 CLS embedding (768-d) and, separately, an HSV histogram (512-d).
  - StandardScaler and PCA(50) were fitted on train, and K-means (n_init 10, seed 42) was fitted on train.
  - k was chosen by maximum train silhouette over k = 2–12 without using labels: frozen ViT gave k* = 8 (silhouette 0.452, k = 6 gives 0.451), and colour histogram gave k* = 12.
- **Alignment with labels,** measured only after fitting:
  - With frozen-ViT features and k = 6, NMI with produce type is **0.950** on train and **0.951** on test (ARI 0.960 / 0.966). Five clusters are pure single-produce clusters.
  - The one mixed cluster is Guava 287 + Gooseberry 67.
  - Alignment with spoilage is weak (NMI 0.13–0.23). The dominant unsupervised structure is produce type, not spoilage.
  - Colour-histogram clusters align much less with produce (NMI ≈ 0.50).
- Clusters are exploratory and are not ground truth.

## XAI — MEASURED
Attention rollout is described in the model card. Panels: `artifacts/experiments/xai/rollout_correct.png` and `rollout_incorrect.png`.
- **Faithfulness check:**
  - Occluding the top-20% rollout patches lowers predicted-class probability by 0.192 on average.
  - Occluding random patches lowers it by 0.020 (Wilcoxon p = 2.3e-30, n = 240).
- **Errors:** in the incorrect cases, attention is on the fruit (not the background) for Spoiled-labelled gooseberries that look fresh. This points to label timing rather than background shortcuts, but it is qualitative only.
